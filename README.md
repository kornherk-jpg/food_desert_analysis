# Access to Healthy Foods and Rates of Diabetes and Obesity 

**Research Question:
How does access to healthy food relate to obesity and diabetes prevalence?**

For this project, I explored how access to healthy foods might be correlated with diabetes and obesity rates in the US.  High rates of obesity and diabetes in parts of the US are driven in part by unequal access to healthy foods.

To gain a better understanding of this topic, I sought to compare the correlation between a measure of healthy food access and rates of diabetes and obesity at the county and the state level. I focused on the Food Environment Index from the 2025 County Health Rankings & Roadmaps data release. The Food Environment Index, which provides rates states and counties on a scale of 1-10 based on access to healthy foods. The index accounts for both access (distance to nearest grocery store, transportation or cost barriers) and income.

I used Excel for initial data cleaning and exploratory visualizations. This included examining the distributions of the different variables of interest to identify patterns. I used  SQL for querying and filtering the dataset and Python for deeper analysis and visualization (using pandas/matplotlib). I explored the correlation between potential predictors (like Food Environment Index) and outcomes (obesity, diabetes rates) at both the county and state level by creating scatterplots and bar charts. After querying the data, I decided to select two states at opposite extremes of the Food Environment Index for a focused comparison.

I found that obesity and diabetes rates tend to decrease as Food Environment Index increases. This pattern holds when comparing counties, states, and the two states chosen for further analysis.  Food Environment Index also appears correlated with poverty rate and racial/economic segregation, suggesting food access is entangled with broader socioeconomic disparities rather than an isolated variable.
